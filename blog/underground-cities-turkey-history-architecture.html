Underground Cities in Turkey: History, Architecture and Visitor Tips

Discover Cappadocia's remarkable underground settlements, hidden passageways, rock-carved chambers and fascinating stories from the past.

Imagine walking beneath the earth's surface and discovering an ancient world of narrow tunnels, rock-carved rooms, hidden passageways and remarkable stories of survival. The Underground Cities in Turkey are among Cappadocia's most fascinating historical attractions, offering insight into how communities used the landscape to create shelter and protect themselves during uncertain times.

Carved into the region's relatively soft volcanic rock, these settlements developed into complex networks of chambers and passageways. Ventilation shafts, storage spaces and heavy stone doors helped people use the underground areas for different purposes. Their design reflects generations of practical knowledge and adaptation.

Whether you enjoy history, architecture or unusual travel experiences, these sites offer a memorable way to explore Turkey's past. Derinkuyu is known for its depth, while Kaymakli is notable for its connected rooms and passages. History of Turkey's Underground Cities

The underground settlements of Cappadocia developed over many centuries. The region's volcanic rock made it possible to carve rooms and passages using relatively simple tools. Over time, people expanded these spaces into interconnected areas for shelter, storage and communal activities.

Underground spaces could provide protection during periods of conflict, invasion and political uncertainty. Narrow passageways and large rolling stone doors made some routes easier to defend, while food-storage areas and ventilation shafts supported the use of the complexes for extended periods.

Christian communities also used underground spaces as places of refuge during parts of the Byzantine period. Some complexes contain chapels, meeting spaces and areas associated with religious life. The history of each site is complex, and the underground cities developed and changed over different periods rather than being built all at once.

Today, these sites help visitors understand how communities adapted architecture to the local geology, environmental conditions and security needs of their time.

1. Derinkuyu Underground City

Derinkuyu is one of Cappadocia's best-known underground cities. The extensive complex descends through multiple levels, with the deepest parts reaching approximately 85 metres below the surface. Not every level or passage is open to visitors.

Within the accessible areas, visitors can see narrow tunnels, storage chambers, ventilation shafts and spaces associated with communal and religious activities. The arrangement shows how an underground settlement could accommodate different needs within a limited area.

Derinkuyu is particularly interesting for visitors curious about ancient engineering and defensive architecture. The narrow passages and low ceilings can make some sections physically demanding, so take your time and follow the designated visitor route.

2. Kaymakli Underground City

Kaymakli is another major underground settlement in Cappadocia, known for its interconnected rooms and maze-like passageways. The complex includes areas associated with food storage, daily activities and animal shelters.

Its layout provides a useful perspective on how people organised practical tasks underground. Rather than relying on one large chamber, the settlement used a network of smaller spaces connected by narrow routes.

Visitors can explore designated sections and observe how the rooms and passages fit together. Like other underground sites, Kaymakli has low ceilings and uneven areas, so comfortable shoes and careful movement are important.

3. Özkonak Underground City

Özkonak offers another perspective on Cappadocia's underground heritage. Its rock-cut chambers and connected passages reflect the importance of controlling access and creating protected spaces.

Compared with the region's most famous underground cities, Özkonak can provide a different experience for travellers interested in exploring more than one historical complex. The details of the layout and accessible areas vary by site, so allow time to understand its particular features rather than expecting every underground city to look the same.

Before visiting, check current opening arrangements, access information and local transport options. A guide can help explain the site's history and the function of its different spaces.

4. Mazı Underground City

Mazı is a smaller underground settlement in Cappadocia, with chambers and passages carved into the rock. It provides another example of how communities made use of the region's geology to create functional underground spaces.

Travellers interested in historical sites beyond the best-known attractions may wish to consider Mazı as part of a broader Cappadocia itinerary. Its scale and visitor experience differ from those of larger complexes such as Derinkuyu and Kaymakli.

Check current access arrangements before adding Mazı to your route. Availability, opening hours and transport options can change, and it is worth confirming these details locally.

Architecture of the Underground Cities

The design of Cappadocia's underground settlements is practical and inventive. The volcanic rock was suitable for carving, while the interconnected chambers allowed people to organise shelter, storage and other activities within a protected environment.

Multi-level construction

Some complexes extend through several levels connected by narrow tunnels and steep stairways. Different chambers served different purposes, including storage, shelter and communal activities. The layout varies from one settlement to another.

Ventilation shafts

Ventilation shafts helped circulate air through underground areas. Their design was an important part of making the spaces usable, particularly where rooms and passageways extended far below ground level.

Rolling stone doors

Large circular stone doors could be moved across certain passageways to restrict access. These features demonstrate how the architecture incorporated defensive measures into the underground layout.

Water wells and storage rooms

Wells and storage chambers helped residents manage essential supplies. Their arrangement reflects the need to organise food and water in places where reaching the surface might not always be possible or safe.

Religious and communal areas

Some underground complexes contain chapels and spaces associated with gathering or worship. These features show that the underground settlements were not simply tunnels for escape but could also support aspects of community life.

Together, these architectural elements reveal how people used the natural properties of volcanic rock to create spaces suited to their needs. The precise features and functions differ across the region's underground settlements.

Best Time to Visit Turkey's Underground Cities

Spring and autumn are generally comfortable seasons for exploring Cappadocia. From April to June and September to October, moderate daytime conditions can make it easier to combine underground sightseeing with walks through the region's valleys and villages.

Summer is also a popular time to visit, but outdoor temperatures can be high. Starting outdoor activities early in the day can help you avoid the strongest afternoon heat. Underground areas may feel cooler than the surface, but conditions vary between sites.

Winter brings the possibility of snow and beautiful scenery, although cold weather or travel disruptions may affect outdoor plans. If you visit during winter, allow flexibility in your schedule.

  • Spring: A good season to combine underground city visits with outdoor exploration.
  • Summer: Plan outdoor activities for cooler hours and carry water.
  • Autumn: Often offers comfortable conditions for exploring the region.
  • Winter: Prepare for cold weather and possible changes to transport or activity schedules.

Always check the latest opening hours, entrance fees, visitor restrictions and transport arrangements before travelling. These details can change and should be confirmed through current official or local sources.

Visitor Tips for a Comfortable Experience

Underground cities are fascinating, but their narrow passages and uneven surfaces require a little preparation. Keep these practical tips in mind to make your visit more comfortable.

Wear comfortable shoes

Choose shoes with good grip. Floors can be uneven, and some routes include steep steps, low ceilings and narrow passageways.

Prepare for confined spaces

Some sections have restricted headroom and tight passages. If you are uncomfortable in enclosed spaces, review the visitor route beforehand and avoid sections that you do not feel comfortable entering.

Allow enough time

A visit may take around one to two hours at a larger site, depending on the accessible route, crowd levels and your interests. Allow additional time for travel between underground cities and other Cappadocia attractions.

Consider a local guide

A knowledgeable guide can explain the history, architecture and cultural significance of the chambers. This can make it easier to understand the differences between sites.

Carry water for outdoor sightseeing

Keep water with you when travelling between attractions, especially during warm weather. Follow site rules about where food and drink are permitted.

Follow safety instructions

Stay on designated visitor routes, watch for low ceilings and avoid restricted areas. Do not enter unstable or closed passages, even if they appear accessible.

Plan transportation

The underground cities are spread across the Cappadocia region. Check distances and transport options before visiting several sites in one day, and leave enough time for your return journey.

Protect the historical structures

Do not carve into rock, touch fragile surfaces unnecessarily, leave rubbish or disturb archaeological features. Follow instructions from site staff to help preserve these historic places.

Travel tip: Derinkuyu and Kaymakli have narrow, low passages. Check the physical demands and accessibility information before visiting, particularly if travelling with young children or anyone who may find steep stairs and confined spaces difficult.

Other Attractions to Explore in Cappadocia

Underground city visits can be combined with other attractions to create a more complete Cappadocia itinerary. The region is known for its rock formations, historic churches, traditional crafts and scenic valleys.

Göreme Open-Air Museum

Explore rock-cut churches and historic religious spaces associated with Cappadocia's Christian heritage. Some interiors contain significant surviving artwork.

Uçhisar Castle

This distinctive rock formation and historic settlement offer wide views over the surrounding landscape. Check access conditions before visiting the upper viewpoints.

Paşabağ Valley

Paşabağ is known for its unusual fairy chimneys and distinctive volcanic rock formations. Walking among these formations provides a different perspective on Cappadocia's geology.

Traditional pottery workshops

The Cappadocia region has a long pottery tradition. A visit to a local workshop can offer insight into traditional techniques and contemporary craft practices.

Cappadocia's valleys

Scenic valleys provide opportunities for walking, photography and appreciating the region's unusual rock formations. Choose routes suited to your fitness and check weather conditions before setting out.

Combining these attractions with underground city visits creates a balanced itinerary that brings together history, nature and local culture. Avoid trying to fit too many distant sites into one day, particularly if you are relying on public transport.

Conclusion

The Underground Cities in Turkey offer a fascinating look at how communities adapted to their environment and created complex spaces beneath the earth. Derinkuyu's extensive levels, Kaymakli's interconnected rooms and the distinctive layouts of Özkonak and Mazı reveal different aspects of Cappadocia's underground heritage.

Ventilation shafts, stone doors, storage areas and communal spaces demonstrate the practical thinking behind these remarkable settlements. Exploring them also offers an opportunity to understand the historical experiences of the people who used them over many centuries.

Plan your visit around the season, check current access information and wear suitable footwear for narrow passages and uneven steps. Combining underground cities with Cappadocia's valleys, rock formations and cultural attractions can make your trip even more rewarding.
