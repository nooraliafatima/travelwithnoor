Most Beautiful Pagodas and Buddhist Temples in Vietnam

Discover historic pagodas, peaceful monasteries, distinctive architecture and the spiritual traditions that shape Vietnam's cultural heritage.

Travel With Noor   |   Vietnam Travel Guide

Vietnam is a wonderful destination for travellers interested in peaceful spiritual places, traditional architecture and cultural history. From pagodas beside serene lakes to temples surrounded by mountains and gardens, its religious landmarks reveal different aspects of the country's heritage.

Visiting the Buddhist Temples in Vietnam offers an opportunity to learn about local traditions, admire intricate craftsmanship and experience places that remain important to worshippers. Some sites are known for their historic towers, while others stand out for their riverside settings, colourful mosaics or quiet gardens.

Each destination has its own story. Hanoi offers lakeside pagodas and historic shrines, Hue is known for its imperial-era heritage and riverside landmarks, and Da Lat features distinctive religious architecture set among hills and pine trees.

1. Tran Quoc Pagoda in Hanoi

Tran Quoc Pagoda is one of Hanoi's best-known religious landmarks. Set on a small peninsula beside West Lake, it is recognised for its red-toned stupa, traditional Buddhist buildings and peaceful waterside surroundings.

The setting makes the pagoda a popular place for photography and quiet reflection. Visitors can admire the architecture, explore the grounds where access is permitted and observe how the site remains part of the city's spiritual life.

Early morning can be a pleasant time to visit, when the lakeside atmosphere is often quieter. As this is an active religious site, dress respectfully and be mindful of worshippers while taking photographs.

2. One Pillar Pagoda in Hanoi

One Pillar Pagoda is one of Hanoi's most distinctive historic structures. Traditionally dated to 1049, the small wooden shrine is supported by a single stone pillar above a pond. Its design is often associated with a lotus flower, a symbol of purity in Buddhist traditions.

The compact structure differs from larger temple complexes, making it particularly interesting for visitors who appreciate architectural details and historical symbolism. The surrounding grounds also provide an opportunity to learn about the site's place in Hanoi's cultural history.

Because the pagoda is a popular attraction, visitors should allow time for other people to move around the site. Follow local instructions and check current access arrangements before visiting.

3. Thien Mu Pagoda in Hue

Thien Mu Pagoda stands beside the Perfume River in Hue and is one of central Vietnam's most recognisable religious landmarks. Founded in 1601, the complex is famous for its seven-storey Phuoc Duyen Tower and traditional buildings set above the river.

The location combines historic architecture with scenic river views. Visitors can explore the grounds, admire the tower and learn about the pagoda's role in Hue's spiritual and cultural history.

A visit can be combined with a boat trip on the Perfume River or other historic attractions in Hue. Allow enough time to appreciate the site rather than treating it as a quick photo stop.

4. Linh Ung Pagoda in Da Nang

Linh Ung Pagoda on the Son Tra Peninsula is known for its coastal setting and impressive white Guanyin statue, which stands approximately 67 metres tall. The statue overlooks the sea and has become one of the area's most recognisable landmarks.

Visitors can explore the temple grounds, admire the sculptures and enjoy broad views across Da Nang's coastline when the weather is clear. The combination of religious architecture, hills and sea scenery makes it an appealing destination for cultural visits and photography.

Conditions on the peninsula can be windy or rainy, and visibility varies. Dress appropriately for a religious site and follow signs around the grounds.

5. Bai Dinh Pagoda in Ninh Binh

Bai Dinh Pagoda is a large religious complex in Ninh Binh, a region famous for limestone formations, rivers and rural scenery. The site combines an older temple area with extensive newer buildings, curved roofs and rows of carved Arhat statues.

The scale of the complex makes it different from the smaller pagodas found in city neighbourhoods. Visitors may need considerable time to walk between different sections, so comfortable footwear is recommended.

Bai Dinh can be combined with visits to nearby landscapes and attractions in Ninh Binh. Consider planning the temple visit alongside a boat trip or countryside exploration, while allowing enough time for transport between locations.

6. Linh Phuoc Pagoda in Da Lat

Linh Phuoc Pagoda is particularly notable for its colourful mosaics made from pieces of glass, ceramics and other materials. Detailed dragon decorations and elaborate surfaces demonstrate the craftsmanship that gives the complex its distinctive appearance.

The temple provides an interesting contrast with more traditional wooden and stone pagodas elsewhere in Vietnam. Visitors can observe the intricate decorative work and appreciate the creativity involved in assembling the mosaic surfaces.

Da Lat's cooler highland setting makes the pagoda a useful stop during a broader exploration of the city. Respect the site's religious purpose and follow any photography restrictions.

7. Truc Lam Zen Monastery in Da Lat

Truc Lam Zen Monastery is set among pine trees near Tuyen Lam Lake, offering a calm environment away from the busiest parts of Da Lat. Its gardens, buildings and natural surroundings create a peaceful setting for visitors interested in Zen Buddhist traditions.

Travellers can walk through permitted areas, admire the landscaped grounds and enjoy views of the surrounding hills. The monastery also provides a chance to learn more about meditation and Zen Buddhism, provided visitors follow local rules and respect the quiet atmosphere.

Consider combining the visit with time near Tuyen Lam Lake. The weather can change in the highlands, so bring a light layer and check conditions before travelling.

8. Tu Hieu Pagoda in Hue

Tu Hieu Pagoda is a historic Buddhist monastery in Hue, surrounded by trees and gardens. Compared with some of the city's more prominent attractions, it can offer a quieter setting for visitors interested in religious heritage and contemplative spaces.

The pagoda is associated with Zen Buddhism and is widely connected with the life of Vietnamese Zen master Thich Nhat Hanh. Its wooded surroundings and traditional buildings make it a meaningful stop for travellers who want to learn about the country's spiritual traditions.

Visitors should move quietly, respect meditation and worship areas, and follow instructions about access. A slower visit can make it easier to appreciate the atmosphere of the monastery.

9. Boi Khe Pagoda near Hanoi

Boi Khe Pagoda is a historic religious site outside central Hanoi. Traditionally dated to the fourteenth century, it offers an opportunity to explore older Vietnamese temple architecture away from some of the capital's busiest visitor areas.

The complex is associated with traditional wooden structures and places of worship. Its history and architectural details can help visitors understand how religious sites developed in rural communities around Hanoi.

If you plan to visit, check the route and local access arrangements in advance. A visit may work best as part of a wider excursion into the surrounding countryside.

10. My Son Sanctuary in Central Vietnam

My Son Sanctuary is an important archaeological site surrounded by green hills and woodland in central Vietnam. Its brick temple towers were built by the Champa civilisation and reflect the region's historic Hindu religious traditions.

Although My Son is not a Buddhist temple complex, it is an important addition to a broader exploration of Vietnam's religious architecture. The surviving structures offer insight into Champa history, building techniques and artistic traditions.

The sanctuary is recognised as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Visitors can explore designated paths, learn about the ruins and appreciate the site's setting. Follow barriers and instructions to help protect the fragile historic structures.

Best Time to Visit Vietnam's Pagodas and Temples

The best time to visit religious landmarks depends on the region, seasonal weather and your wider itinerary. Vietnam stretches across different climate zones, so conditions in Hanoi may differ from those in Hue, Da Nang or Da Lat.

Northern Vietnam

Spring and autumn can offer comfortable conditions for exploring Hanoi and its surroundings. Temperatures and rainfall vary, so check the forecast before planning outdoor visits.

Central Vietnam

Hue and Da Nang can be pleasant to explore during drier periods, but seasonal rain and storms can affect travel plans. Check regional weather updates when choosing dates.

Da Lat and the Central Highlands

Da Lat is known for its cooler climate compared with many lowland destinations. Rain can affect outdoor sightseeing during wetter periods, so a flexible schedule is useful.

Ninh Binh

Spring and autumn are often attractive times to explore Ninh Binh's temple complexes and surrounding countryside. If you plan to combine temple visits with boat trips, check conditions for both activities.

Religious sites may adjust opening arrangements for ceremonies, festivals, restoration work or other reasons. Confirm current access details before travelling.

Tips for Visiting Temples and Pagodas in Vietnam

A little preparation can make visits to Vietnam's religious landmarks more comfortable and respectful. Keep these practical points in mind as you plan your route.

Dress respectfully

Choose modest clothing that covers your shoulders and knees where appropriate. Follow posted rules about footwear, clothing and access to religious buildings.

Respect worshippers

Speak quietly, avoid interrupting ceremonies and do not enter restricted areas. Some parts of a complex may be reserved for monks, residents or worshippers.

Ask before taking photographs

Photography may be restricted inside shrines or during religious ceremonies. Look for signs and ask staff when unsure, particularly before photographing people at prayer.

Plan transport and travel time

Some sites are located outside major city centres. Check distances, transport options and expected travel times before combining multiple temples in one day.

Carry water and suitable footwear

Large complexes can involve considerable walking, stairs and uneven surfaces. Comfortable shoes and water are helpful, particularly in warm weather.

Protect cultural heritage

Avoid touching fragile carvings, climbing on historic structures or leaving rubbish behind. Follow the site's rules to help preserve these places for future visitors and local communities.

Travel tip: Some locations in this guide, including My Son Sanctuary, represent religious traditions other than Buddhism. Visiting them alongside Buddhist pagodas provides a broader understanding of Vietnam's diverse architectural and spiritual heritage.

Conclusion

Vietnam's pagodas and religious landmarks offer more than beautiful architecture. They provide insight into spiritual traditions, historic craftsmanship and the cultural identities of different regions. From the lakeside setting of Tran Quoc Pagoda in Hanoi to the riverside Thien Mu Pagoda in Hue and the colourful mosaics of Linh Phuoc Pagoda in Da Lat, each site offers a distinctive experience.

Exploring the Buddhist Temples in Vietnam can add depth to a journey, offering opportunities to learn about local customs and appreciate peaceful historic settings. Adding places such as My Son Sanctuary also helps travellers understand the country's wider religious and architectural history.
