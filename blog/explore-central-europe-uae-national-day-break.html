How to Explore Central Europe During the UAE National Day Break

Discover festive Christmas markets, historic cities, grand palaces and easy train routes across Central Europe.

Home / Blog / Central Europe During UAE National Day
Travel Guide | Europe | Winter Travel

The UAE National Day holiday falls on Wednesday 2 and Thursday 3 December 2026. Take Friday 4 December as leave and, with the weekend, you get five days off in a row. That is enough for a proper first look at Central Europe: old towns, grand palaces, river views and, by early December, Christmas markets already in full swing.

The region works well for a short break because the cities sit close together. Prague, Vienna, Bratislava and Budapest are linked by direct trains, so you spend your time in the cities rather than in transit. Many travellers in Dubai choose National Day packages to Europe from Dubai for exactly this reason: flights, hotels and city-to-city transfers are arranged in one booking, and the few days you have go on sightseeing rather than logistics.

Here is how to plan it, from which cities to pick to what to pack and what to sort out before you fly.

Why Central Europe Suits the National Day Break

One short trip gives you several countries, each with its own language, food and architecture. Austria, the Czech Republic, Hungary, Slovakia and Germany are all in the Schengen area, so moving between them is easy. Between them you get castle hills, imperial palaces, riverside promenades and thermal baths.

Timing helps too. By 2 December every major market in the table below is open, so you can see these cities at their most festive.

Vienna Rathausplatz Christkindlmarkt (Schönbrunn Palace market runs longer) 13 Nov – 26 Dec (Schönbrunn: 6 Nov – 6 Jan 2027)

Prague, Czech Republic: Cobbled Lanes and Castle Views

Prague is the easiest place to start. The Old Town is compact, so most of the big sights are a short walk apart. Old Town Square is the heart of it, with its pastel facades, the twin spires of Týn Church and the Astronomical Clock, which has been on the Town Hall wall since 1410. Every hour from 9 am to 11 pm, twelve apostle figures parade past the clock's windows. You can watch for free from the square, so arrive a few minutes early to get a spot.

Prague Castle is the other must-see. It is a whole complex of courtyards and palaces, with St Vitus Cathedral at its centre, and it looks out over the city from the hill. From there, walk down and cross Charles Bridge over the Vltava. Go early in the morning if you want to see it without the crowds.

In early December the Old Town Square market adds a huge Christmas tree and stalls selling crafts, roast chestnuts and mulled wine. When you get hungry, look for a traditional pub serving roast pork or duck with Czech dumplings.

Vienna, Austria: Imperial Palaces and Coffee Houses

Vienna suits anyone who likes history, classical music and grand architecture. Schönbrunn Palace is the headline sight. It was the summer residence of the Habsburg rulers, and its state rooms and formal gardens give a real sense of how the imperial court lived.

Music is part of daily life here. Book a concert at a historic hall such as the Musikverein or the State Opera, or simply linger in a traditional coffee house, a tradition Vienna is proud of. The Christkindlmarkt in front of the City Hall (Rathausplatz) is the best known of the city's Christmas markets. The market at Schönbrunn Palace is open during the day and into the evening, so it combines easily with a palace visit.

Budapest, Hungary: The Danube and the Thermal Baths

The Danube splits Budapest in two: hilly Buda on the west bank and flat, busy Pest on the east. Walk the riverfront promenade in Pest and you will pass the Hungarian Parliament, one of the most striking buildings in Europe. St Stephen's Basilica is a short walk inland, and its square hosts one of the city's two main Christmas markets, the other being at Vörösmarty tér.

Budapest is also known for its thermal baths, and winter is a good time to use them. Széchenyi Baths is open daily, and its outdoor pools stay warm in the cold air. One update to know about: the Gellért Baths are closed for renovation, so do not plan your trip around them.

For food, try goulash, a paprika-rich stew, and kürtőskalács, the sweet chimney cake baked over coals at the market stalls.

Bratislava, Slovakia: An Easy Stop Between Vienna and Budapest

Bratislava is smaller than its neighbours, and that is part of its appeal. The Old Town is compact and relaxed, with small squares, cafés and a castle above the Danube. It is also very close to Vienna, only about an hour by train, while Budapest is roughly two and a half hours further on. That makes it a handy stopover for a few hours or a night rather than a destination that needs several days.

The Christmas market fills Main Square and Hviezdoslav Square. It is open daily from 10 am to 10 pm, apart from 24 and 25 December, and it tends to feel cosier and cheaper than Vienna's.

Germany: Munich, Berlin or Dresden

Germany is the natural add-on if you have more than five days. Pick one city rather than trying to fit in all three.

Munich has the most traditional feel, with Bavarian architecture, big museums and Marienplatz at its centre, where the New Town Hall dominates the square. The city's Christkindlmarkt is held right there on Marienplatz, and Nymphenburg Palace, once a summer residence of the Bavarian rulers, is worth a half day if you like royal history.

Berlin is a different experience. You can see the Brandenburg Gate, visit the Berlin Wall memorials and spend hours in the city's museums. Dresden, a smaller option not far north of Prague, is known for its rebuilt historic centre and baroque architecture.

Build a Route That Follows the Map

The simplest plan is a straight line: Prague, Vienna, Bratislava, Budapest. Each leg is a few hours at most by train, so you avoid wasting a day on every transfer.

Train Route Approximate Journey Time
Prague to Vienna About 4 hours
Vienna to Bratislava About 1 hour
Bratislava to Budapest About 2 hours 20 minutes to 2.5 hours
Vienna to Budapest (direct, fastest Railjet) About 2 hours 20 minutes

With five days, choose two cities and enjoy them rather than rushing. Vienna and Budapest work well together, and so do Prague and Vienna. Add Bratislava as a half-day stop on the way. If you can take more leave, add the other city and put Munich or another German city at the end. You can also fly into one city and out of another, which saves you backtracking. The order is flexible, so let flight times and hotel prices decide it.

What to Expect from the Weather in Early December

It will feel very different from the UAE. In early December, daytime highs in these cities are typically around 2 to 6°C, and nights hover around freezing. Rain, fog and cold wind are common, and snow is possible. Daylight is short too, with the sun setting at around 4 pm, so plan outdoor sights for the middle of the day and save markets and museums for the evening.

Pack a warm waterproof coat, gloves, a scarf and a hat. Choose comfortable shoes with good grip, because cobbled streets get slippery when wet or icy. Layers work best: trams, cafés and museums are properly warm indoors, so you will want to take things off as you go.

Eat Well and Shop at the Markets

Food is one of the easiest ways to feel the difference between these countries. In Prague, go for dumplings with roast meat. In Vienna, order Wiener Schnitzel and finish with apple strudel in a traditional coffee house. In Budapest, try goulash and a warm chimney cake. In Bratislava, look for Slovak potato dishes such as halušky.

The Christmas markets are also the best place to shop. You will find handmade decorations, wooden toys, local crafts and winter-themed gifts. Stalls on the main squares tend to charge more than those in the neighbourhood markets, so it can pay to walk a little further.

Budget, Visas and Booking

Start your budget with the big items: international flights, hotels, trains between cities, food, attraction tickets, travel insurance and shopping. Currencies differ along the route. Austria, Slovakia and Germany use the euro, while the Czech Republic uses the koruna and Hungary the forint.

Hotels in the central areas cost more, but they save time and taxi fares. Early December is Christmas market season, so book rooms and train tickets well ahead.

Visas depend on your passport, not on your UAE residency. UAE passport holders can visit the Schengen area without a visa, while most other nationalities need a Schengen visa, applied for through the country where you will spend most nights. Start early, because processing can take weeks. The EU's new ETIAS travel authorisation is not yet in operation and has no confirmed start date, so there is nothing to apply for at the moment. Border crossings now also use the Entry/Exit System, which records fingerprints and a photo, so allow a little extra time at passport control.

Conclusion

Central Europe packs a lot into a short break: Prague's medieval streets, Vienna's palaces, Budapest's thermal baths and Bratislava's quiet Old Town, all with Christmas markets lighting up the evenings. Pick your cities, dress for the cold, plan your transport and check your visa position early, and the National Day holiday can become a trip you remember for years.
