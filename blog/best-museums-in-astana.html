Best Museums to Visit in Astana for Art, History and Culture

Discover Kazakhstan's history, literature, military heritage, science and contemporary art.

Astana is best known for its futuristic skyline, glass towers and impressive architectural landmarks. However, Kazakhstan's capital also has a fascinating collection of museums that explain the country's history, culture and development.

From ancient steppe civilizations and national independence to literature, military history and contemporary art, Astana's museums offer a deeper understanding of Kazakhstan beyond its modern buildings.

Each museum has a different focus, making it easy to choose attractions according to your interests. They also fit naturally into a Kazakhstan holiday from Dubai alongside the capital's famous monuments, shopping areas and scenic attractions.

Whether you're travelling with friends, family or independently, these museums provide an interesting way to explore Astana's cultural side.

National Museum of the Republic of Kazakhstan

If you have time to visit only one museum in Astana, the National Museum of the Republic of Kazakhstan is an excellent starting point. Located on Tauelsizdik Avenue near Independence Square, it opened in 2014 and is one of the largest museums in Central Asia.

Its exhibitions cover ancient and medieval history, ethnography, modern Kazakhstan and the development of Astana. The different halls provide an introduction to the country's cultural heritage and its journey towards independence.

Explore the Gold Hall

The Gold Hall is one of the museum's most memorable sections. It features the Golden Man, a reconstruction of the Saka warrior discovered in the Issyk burial mound near Almaty in 1969–70.

The intricate gold decorations demonstrate the craftsmanship of ancient nomadic communities. The original archaeological finds are kept separately at Astana's Museum of Gold and Precious Metals.

The National Museum also includes modern art exhibitions, making it an interesting destination for visitors who enjoy both history and creative works.

Allow around two to four hours for your visit. The museum is described as closed on Mondays, so check its current schedule before planning your trip.

Museum of the First President

The Museum of the First President focuses on Kazakhstan's political development after independence in 1991. It explores the establishment of state institutions, the country's early independence years and the development of its new capital.

Dedicated to Nursultan Nazarbayev, Kazakhstan's first president, the museum is part of the Nazarbayev Center complex on Bokeikhan Street.

Exhibitions include photographs, official documents, state awards and gifts presented by foreign leaders. These displays provide a look at Kazakhstan's political history and its international relationships.

The museum is particularly suitable for visitors interested in modern history, government and the decisions that shaped the country after independence.

Entry is described as free, and visitors may need to show identification. Bring your passport and confirm the current opening hours before visiting.

Saken Seifullin Museum

The Saken Seifullin Museum is dedicated to one of Kazakhstan's important literary and cultural figures. Seifullin was a poet, writer and public figure who contributed to modern Kazakh literature and the political and cultural life of the early twentieth century.

The museum is located at 20 Mukhtar Auezov Street, inside an old wooden log building dating from the nineteenth century.

Although Seifullin worked in the building, he did not live there. Its historic architecture gives the museum a different atmosphere from Astana's modern glass towers.

Six exhibition halls contain more than 5,000 items, including manuscripts, photographs, books and personal belongings.

This museum is especially interesting for visitors who enjoy literature, cultural history and traditional architecture. It offers a quieter experience focused on the life and work of a significant Kazakh writer.

Military History Museum of the Armed Forces

The Military History Museum of the Armed Forces opened in December 2015. Located on Respublika Avenue, it occupies a blue-and-white building with a T-34 tank displayed outside.

The museum contains more than 6,000 exhibits covering Kazakhstan's military history from ancient times to the present.

Its exhibition halls explore ancient warfare, twentieth-century conflicts, modern weapons and the development of the armed forces.

Visitors can see uniforms, weapons, medals, photographs and historical documents. A fine arts hall also features works connected with military themes.

Admission is described as free, and the museum is closed on Mondays. Many displays are in Kazakh and Russian, so a translation app may be useful for international visitors.

The museum can be an interesting stop for older children and adults interested in history, military technology and national heritage.

Nur Alem Museum of Future Energy

Nur Alem is one of Astana's most distinctive architectural attractions. The giant glass sphere was built for Expo 2017 and stands approximately 100 metres tall and 80 metres wide.

The building now houses the Museum of Future Energy, where exhibitions explore different forms of energy and the possibilities of future technology.

Explore the Energy Exhibitions

The museum's displays cover solar, wind, water, biomass and kinetic energy. Another floor presents ideas about Astana's future.

Interactive exhibits make the museum suitable for adults and children interested in science, engineering and technology.

The sphere is also impressive from the outside. Its unusual shape and illuminated appearance make it a distinctive landmark, especially around sunset.

Visitors interested in modern architecture and technology can combine Nur Alem with other attractions in the city.

Museum of the History of Kazakhstan Railways

The Museum of the History of Kazakhstan Railways opened in June 2026 beside Nurly Zhol railway station.

Railways have played an important role in connecting Kazakhstan's vast territory, supporting trade and linking different regions.

The museum explores this history through more than 250 exhibits, including railway lanterns, signalling equipment, tools, archive materials and technical devices from different periods.

What Can You See?

Its four themed halls cover locomotives, railway professions, modern technologies and the development of railway transport.

The museum is a useful stop for visitors interested in engineering, transport and industrial history.

Kazakhstan's older Central Museum of Railway Transport is located in Almaty, so visitors should distinguish it from this museum in Astana when planning their itinerary.

Contemporary Art in Astana: Kulanshi and Beyond

Astana's contemporary art scene is smaller than Almaty's, but it offers interesting places to explore Kazakh and international creative work.

Kulanshi Contemporary Art Center

Founded in 2006, Kulanshi Contemporary Art Center is located on the sixth floor of the Palace of Peace and Reconciliation.

The 62-metre glass pyramid was designed by Norman Foster and is one of Astana's recognisable architectural landmarks.

Kulanshi's exhibition halls feature painting, sculpture, photography and other artistic forms. Its programme includes work by Kazakh and international artists.

Forte Kulanshi Art Space

Kulanshi also operates Forte Kulanshi Art Space on Dostyk Street, which focuses on contemporary Kazakh art.

Exhibition programmes change regularly, so check the current schedule before visiting.

Visitors interested in modern art can also explore the National Museum's modern art halls for an additional cultural experience.

Tips for Visiting Museums in Astana

Astana's museums cover different aspects of Kazakhstan's history and culture, so planning your visits around your interests can make your itinerary more enjoyable.

Start with the National Museum

The National Museum provides a broad introduction to Kazakhstan's history and cultural heritage. It is a good starting point before visiting museums focused on specific subjects.

Choose Museums According to Your Interests

  • National history: Visit the National Museum of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
  • Modern political history: Explore the Museum of the First President.
  • Literature: Visit the Saken Seifullin Museum.
  • Military history: Explore the Military History Museum.
  • Science and technology: Include Nur Alem Museum of Future Energy.
  • Transport history: Visit the Museum of the History of Kazakhstan Railways.
  • Contemporary art: Check exhibitions at Kulanshi and Forte Kulanshi Art Space.

Check Opening Days and Ticket Details

Confirm opening hours, admission requirements and current exhibition schedules before travelling to a museum.

Several museums, including the National Museum and Military History Museum, are described as closed on Mondays. Opening days and visitor arrangements can change, so check directly before your visit.

Consider Guided Tours

Some museums offer guided tours that can help visitors understand historical exhibits and cultural references.

Since many displays are in Kazakh or Russian, a guide or translation app can be useful for international travellers.

Wear Comfortable Shoes

Larger museums involve considerable walking. Comfortable shoes and enough time between attractions can make the experience easier, especially when visiting several museums in one day.

Explore Art in Almaty Too

If your Kazakhstan itinerary also includes Almaty, consider visiting the Abilkhan Kasteev National Museum of Arts.

It is located in Almaty rather than Astana and is known as the country's largest fine art museum.

Conclusion

Astana offers more than futuristic architecture and modern landmarks. Its museums provide an opportunity to explore Kazakhstan's ancient steppe history, independence, literature, military heritage, railway development and future energy ideas.

The National Museum, Saken Seifullin Museum and Nur Alem are excellent starting points for visitors interested in the country's history, culture and modern development.

Other museums offer more specialised experiences, from political history and military collections to contemporary art and engineering.

Choose attractions according to your interests, check opening days and allow enough time to explore. With a little planning, Astana's museums can become some of the most rewarding stops on a cultural trip to Kazakhstan.
