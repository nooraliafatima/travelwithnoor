Best Museums to Visit in Astana for Art, History and Culture

Discover the museums that reveal Kazakhstan's history, literature, military heritage, contemporary art and future energy.

Home / Blog / Best Museums in Astana
Travel Guide | Kazakhstan | Museums and Culture

Astana is best known for its skyline, all-glass towers and futuristic landmarks. But the capital of Kazakhstan also has a good set of museums that explain how the country got here: the ancient steppe cultures, the road to independence, the writers and soldiers who shaped national identity, and the ideas now driving its future. If you're planning a cultural trip, they're a smart way to add depth to the sightseeing.

Each museum has a different focus, from national history to literature, military history and contemporary art. They fit neatly into a Kazakhstan holiday itinerary from Dubai alongside the famous monuments, shopping malls and scenic day trips. Whether you're travelling with friends, family or on your own, here's where to spend your time.

National Museum of the Republic of Kazakhstan

If you only have time for one museum, make it this one. The National Museum is on Tauelsizdik Avenue, near Independence Square, and it opened in 2014 as one of the largest museums in Central Asia. Its halls take you from ancient and medieval history through ethnography to the story of independent Kazakhstan, with a hall on Astana itself.

The Gold Hall is the highlight for many visitors, and it features the Golden Man, a reconstruction of the Saka warrior found in the Issyk burial mound near Almaty in 1969–70. The intricate gold work shows how skilled the ancient nomadic craftspeople were. The original finds are kept separately at Astana's Museum of Gold and Precious Metals.

The museum also has halls of modern art, so it covers more than history. Allow two to four hours, and note that it's closed on Mondays.

Museum of the First President

This museum covers the political story of modern Kazakhstan: the years after independence in 1991, the establishment of state institutions and the building of the new capital. It's dedicated to Nursultan Nazarbayev, the country's first president, and it's part of the Nazarbayev Center complex on Bokeikhan Street.

Exhibits include photographs, official documents, state awards and gifts from foreign leaders, which show how the country built relationships abroad. It's a good fit if you want to understand the political decisions behind modern Kazakhstan.

Entry is free, and you may need to show ID, so bring your passport. Confirm opening hours before you go.

Saken Seifullin Museum

The Saken Seifullin Museum is the largest museum in Kazakhstan devoted to a writer. Seifullin was a poet, writer and public figure who played a major part in the development of modern Kazakh literature and in the cultural and political life of the early twentieth century.

The museum is at 20 Mukhtar Auezov Street, in an old wooden log building that dates back to the 19th century. Seifullin worked in this building, though he didn't live there.

Six halls hold more than 5,000 items, including manuscripts, photographs, books and personal belongings. It feels very different from the glass towers elsewhere in the city, which is part of its charm, especially for literature and architecture lovers.

Military History Museum of the Armed Forces

Opened in December 2015, the Military History Museum is on Respublika Avenue in a blue-and-white building, with a T-34 tank outside. More than 6,000 exhibits cover Kazakhstan's military history from antiquity to the present, in halls on ancient warfare, 20th-century conflicts, modern weapons and the armed forces.

There's also a fine arts hall with works on military themes. You'll find uniforms, weapons, medals, documents and photographs.

Admission is free, and it's closed on Mondays. Many labels are in Kazakh and Russian, so a translation app helps. Older children interested in history or technology tend to enjoy it.

Nur Alem Museum of Future Energy

One of Astana's most distinctive attractions is the giant glass sphere built for Expo 2017. Nur Alem is 100 metres tall and 80 metres wide, with eight floors, and it's the world's largest spherical building. It now houses the Museum of Future Energy, with exhibits on solar, wind, water, biomass and kinetic energy, plus a floor imagining Astana in the future.

The displays are interactive, so it works well for both adults and children who like science and technology. The sphere looks striking from the outside too, especially around sunset when the lights come on.

Museum of the History of Kazakhstan Railways

This is one of the capital's newest museums, opened in June 2026 beside the Nurly Zhol railway station. Railways have been central to the development of Kazakhstan's huge territory, linking regions and driving trade, and the museum tells that story through more than 250 exhibits.

The collection includes railway lanterns, signalling equipment, tools, archive materials and technical devices from different eras.

It has four themed halls on locomotives, railway professions, modern technologies and the history of the railways, and it's a decent stop for anyone interested in engineering, transport or industrial history. The country's older Central Museum of Railway Transport is in Almaty, so don't confuse the two.

Contemporary Art in Astana: Kulanshi and Beyond

Astana's art scene is smaller than Almaty's, but it's worth a look. Kulanshi Contemporary Art Center, founded in 2006, is on the sixth floor of the Palace of Peace and Reconciliation, the 62-metre glass pyramid designed by Norman Foster.

Its exhibition halls show work by Kazakh and international artists across painting, sculpture, photography and other forms, and the programme changes regularly.

Kulanshi also runs the Forte Kulanshi Art Space on Dostyk Street, a gallery focused on current Kazakh art. Exhibitions change often, so check the current programme before you visit. If you want more, the National Museum's modern art halls are a good complement.

Tips for Visiting Museums in Astana

Start at the National Museum for the big picture of Kazakhstan's history. From there, the Saken Seifullin Museum adds the literary side, and the Military History Museum covers defence and independence. If you're also visiting Almaty, that's where you'll find the Abilkhan Kasteev National Museum of Arts, the country's biggest fine art museum; it isn't in Astana.

Check opening days and ticket details before you travel. Several of these museums, including the National Museum and the Military History Museum, close on Mondays. Some offer guided tours, which help if the labels are only in Kazakh or Russian, and the bigger museums involve a lot of walking, so wear comfortable shoes.

Final Thoughts

Astana has far more to offer than its architecture. Its museums take you through the ancient steppe, independence, literature, defence, railways and the future of energy, with a contemporary art scene running alongside. Each one shows a different side of the country.

The National Museum, the Saken Seifullin Museum and Nur Alem make a good starting trio, and the rest depend on your interests. Check the opening days, plan your route and give yourself time, and the capital can turn out to be one of the most rewarding stops on your Kazakhstan trip.
