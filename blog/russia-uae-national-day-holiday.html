What Can You Experience in Russia During the UAE National Day Holiday?

Discover Moscow's famous landmarks, Saint Petersburg's imperial architecture and the atmosphere of a Russian winter.

The UAE National Day break (2–3 December) is a good chance for UAE residents to get away from the routine and see somewhere completely different. Russia pairs historic landmarks, grand architecture, museums, shopping and real winter weather. Moscow and Saint Petersburg together make an easy two-city trip, with palaces, cathedrals, old streets and modern entertainment. Russia National Day Holiday Packages can bundle flights, hotels, sightseeing and transfers, which takes much of the planning off your hands.

Expect a big change from UAE weather. Early December in Moscow and Saint Petersburg is properly cold, with average temperatures around -3°C. You might get snow, sleet or rain, so pack warm layers. December is also low season, so the big sights are often less crowded than in summer.

Experience Russia During the UAE National Day Holiday

1. Red Square, Moscow

Red Square is Moscow’s best-known spot and the natural place to begin. You’ll find the colourful domes of Saint Basil’s Cathedral, the Kremlin walls and the elegant GUM department store all within a short walk. In winter, the surrounding buildings look even more dramatic against the cold air and early dusk.

It’s more than a photo stop. The square packs several landmarks and a lot of Russian history into one place, and it’s especially atmospheric after dark when everything is lit up.

2. The Moscow Kremlin

The Kremlin isn’t a single tower but a large fortified complex at the heart of the city, with cathedrals, palaces and walls that tell the story of Russian politics, religion and architecture. Visitors can walk the grounds and see the cathedrals on Cathedral Square.

One planning note matters here: the Kremlin museums, including the Armoury Chamber and the Cathedral Square museums, are closed on Thursdays, and 3 December 2026 falls on a Thursday. Cathedral Square museums are open 10:00–17:00 at this time of year, so plan your Kremlin visit for another day and check the schedule and ticket requirements in advance.

3. The Moscow Metro

The Metro is worth riding even if you don’t need it. Several stations look like underground palaces, with chandeliers, columns, mosaics, sculptures and murals. Stations like Komsomolskaya, Mayakovskaya, Kievskaya and Ploshchad Revolyutsii are popular among visitors.

It’s practical in winter too, since it lets you move between areas such as Red Square and Arbat Street without spending long outside in the cold.

4. Arbat Street

Arbat Street shows you a more relaxed side of Moscow. This pedestrian street is lined with cafés, restaurants, shops, souvenir stalls and street performers. It’s a good place to slow down between the big sights, pick up some Russian souvenirs, try the local food and watch daily life go by.

5. Saint Petersburg’s Imperial Beauty

Saint Petersburg is the place to see Russia’s imperial past and its European-style architecture. The city is known for palaces, canals, cathedrals, museums and wide boulevards, and the historic centre has plenty of photography spots.

You can reach it from Moscow by high-speed Sapsan train, which takes roughly 3.5 to 4 hours and is a handy alternative to flying. Many packages combine both cities.

6. The Hermitage Museum and Winter Palace

The Hermitage is one of Saint Petersburg’s biggest cultural draws, and much of it is housed in the Winter Palace, a former imperial residence. The collection is huge, covering art and historical artefacts from many centuries, and the palace interiors are impressive in themselves.

Early December has very short days, with the sun rising after 9:30 am and setting around 4 pm, so indoor attractions like the Hermitage are a smart way to use the daylight hours. The museum is closed on Mondays, so plan around that.

7. Saint Isaac’s Cathedral and Other Landmarks

Saint Isaac’s Cathedral is one of the city’s most recognisable buildings, with its huge dome, columns and mosaics. You can add the Church of the Savior on Spilled Blood, Kazan Cathedral and the Peter and Paul Fortress to your list.

The weather can be cold and damp, so schedule your outdoor sightseeing carefully and give yourself time to warm up between stops.

8. A Russian Winter Experience

Part of the appeal is the winter itself, which feels nothing like the UAE. Streets and buildings are lit up, and New Year decorations start to appear in December, although Russia celebrates Christmas in early January, not on 25 December.

Don’t count on snow every day. It can be cold, cloudy, snowy or rainy, sometimes all in the same trip. Waterproof footwear, warm layers, gloves and a proper winter jacket will make outdoor sightseeing much easier.

9. Russian Food to Try

Food is a big part of the experience. Look out for borscht, pelmeni, blini, pirozhki, beef stroganoff and syrniki, plus plenty of pastries and hot drinks. Both cities have traditional restaurants, modern cafés and international options.

Hearty soups and dumplings are especially welcome on a cold evening. If you have dietary needs, check menus and ingredients before ordering.

10. Shopping and Evenings Out

Moscow has large shopping centres, department stores and souvenir shops. GUM, beside Red Square, is as much about the historic building as the shopping. Saint Petersburg is good for souvenirs, local crafts, sweets and gifts.

In the evening, you can stroll the lit-up streets, have dinner, catch a cultural performance, or simply relax at the hotel with a warm drink. Daylight is short, particularly in Saint Petersburg, where the sun sets around 4 pm, so do your outdoor sightseeing early.

Visas and Practical Tips

Visa rules depend on your passport, not your UAE residency. UAE citizens can enter Russia visa-free for up to 90 days within any 180-day period. Other UAE residents generally need a visa, and Russia’s e-visa covers dozens of nationalities, including Indian passport holders. Check the current rules for your nationality before booking, and make sure your UAE residence visa is valid beyond your return date.

Direct flights between the UAE and Moscow are frequent, but it’s worth confirming schedules and payment options with your tour operator before you go, because some international bank cards don’t work in Russia.

Why Choose Russia for the National Day Holiday?

Russia offers something different: history, culture, architecture, food, shopping, museums and winter scenery in one trip. Visiting two main cities gives you a big change of scene, with Saint Petersburg’s palaces and museums on one side and Moscow’s landmarks and city energy on the other.

Six or seven days is enough to see the highlights of both cities without rushing. Packages that include transfers between the two make the trip smoother, and a good mix of indoor and outdoor plans keeps the cold from getting in the way.

Celebrate UAE National Day Before Your Russian Holiday

The UAE National Day holiday is a chance to celebrate the country’s heritage and unity before setting off on an international adventure. Enjoy the celebrations at home, then prepare for a change of scenery with Russia’s historic landmarks, museums and winter atmosphere.

Final Thoughts

UAE National Day in Russia can be a memorable international holiday, from Red Square and the Kremlin in Moscow to the Hermitage and cathedrals of Saint Petersburg. The cold is a refreshing contrast to the UAE, as long as you pack properly.

A little planning goes a long way. Pick comfortable hotels, arrange transport between the cities, confirm entry requirements and bring proper winter clothing. For anyone looking at UAE National Day travel packages, Russia is a distinctive choice.
