Albanian Alps Travel Guide: Best Places to Visit

Discover Theth, Valbona, Vermosh, Lëpushë, Razëm and other spectacular places in Albania's dramatic northern mountains.

Aerial view of the Albanian Alps, including the Theth and Valbona areas. Photo by Albinfo, licensed under CC BY-SA 3.0 .

The Albanian Alps, in the far north of the country, are one of the most dramatic mountain landscapes in the Balkans. Locals call them the Accursed Mountains (Bjeshkët e Nemuna), and after one look at the jagged peaks and deep valleys, it's easy to see why. Between the ridgelines are villages like Theth, Valbona, Vermosh, Lëpushë and Razëm, along with waterfalls, forests and some of the best hiking trails in the region.

Theth

Theth is the best-known base in the Albanian Alps, and it earns the reputation. The village sits in a valley ringed by peaks, forest and alpine meadow, and the road in is part of the experience: it climbs over the Thore Pass (Qafa e Thorës), at around 1,700 to 1,800 metres depending on the source, one of the highest paved roads in Albania. The road is usually closed by snow from November to May, so plan around that if you're travelling outside summer.

The village itself is known for its traditional stone houses and guesthouses, which give a real sense of northern Albanian hospitality. The Kulla e Ngujimit, a stone "lock-in tower" once used as a refuge under the region's old blood-feud customs, is one of the more unusual sights, and the 19th-century village church is worth a look too. Theth has grown into a serious hiking base while keeping its village character.

Grunas Waterfall

Grunas Waterfall is an easy walk from Theth, through forest and along a rugged mountain trail. It's fuller and more dramatic after rain or snowmelt, so spring and early summer usually give you the best flow. Trail conditions can change quickly in the mountains, so decent hiking shoes are worth having. Grunas Canyon, nearby, is another good stop if you want more time outdoors.

Blue Eye of Theth

The Blue Eye of Theth (Syri i Kaltër) is a deep spring pool, startlingly blue, tucked into the forest a walk from the village. Most people combine it with the Grunas walk. The water is genuinely cold, so a swim is bracing rather than relaxing, but plenty of hikers take the plunge anyway. It's a quiet, shaded spot and a natural rest stop partway through a longer day.

Valbona Valley

Valbona Valley, on the other side of the mountains from Theth, is protected as Valbona Valley National Park, Albania's largest protected area at around 80 square kilometres. It's built around the Valbona River, whose water runs remarkably clear and cold, and it's surrounded by some of the highest peaks in the country.

You can drive in, stay at a guesthouse, or use it as a base for hiking, fishing or simply enjoying mountain life at a slower pace. The valley has caves worth exploring too, including one near the village of Dragobi.

Valbona Pass

The hike over the Valbona Pass (Qafa e Valbonës, about 1,800 metres) is the classic route between Theth and Valbona, and one of the most popular treks in the Albanian Alps. It follows an old mule trail once used to connect the two valleys, and most hikers cover it in around 6 to 8 hours, with roughly 1,000 metres of ascent.

The trail is steep in the upper section but well marked, and there are usually a couple of places to rest and buy a drink along the way. Start early, check the weather first, and carry enough water and layers, since conditions on an exposed mountain pass can shift fast. The views back over both valleys from the top make the climb worth it.

Vermosh

Vermosh sits in the far north of Albania, in the Kelmend region near the borders with Montenegro. It has the same highland scenery as Theth and Valbona: mountain valleys, forest, meadow and small rural villages, but with far fewer visitors.

It's a good pick for hiking, mountaineering and quiet landscape photography, and a solid base for exploring the wider Kelmend region if you want outdoor adventure alongside a look at traditional village life.

Lëpushë

Lëpushë, also in Kelmend, is a small mountain village known for its peaceful setting among high peaks, meadows and forest. It's especially green and lush in summer, when the alpine pastures fill out.

There's good hiking around the village, without the crowds that gather at Theth and Valbona in high season. Lëpushë combines naturally with Vermosh for a two- or three-day loop through Kelmend.

Razëm

Razëm is a small mountain area near Shkodër, with pine and fir forest and easy alpine scenery. It's a gentler introduction to the mountains than Theth or Valbona, and the drive there is straightforward.

It suits hiking, short excursions and winter activities, and it's easy to pair with a visit to Shkodër itself. If you want a taste of the Albanian Alps without a serious trek, Razëm is a reasonable starting point.

Maja e Jezercës

Maja e Jezercës, at 2,694 metres, is the highest peak in the Albanian Alps and the second-highest in Albania. It sits between the Theth and Valbona valleys, and reaching the summit is a serious undertaking. Most guides recommend splitting it over two days, and it's for experienced, well-equipped mountaineers only.

You don't need to summit to appreciate it, though. Several trails and viewpoints in Theth and Valbona give you a good look at Jezerca's steep ridges and rocky faces, which is more than enough for most visitors.

Gashi River Valley

The Gashi River Valley is one of the wilder, less-visited corners of northern Albania, known for its forests, river scenery and remoteness. Part of its old-growth beech forest is protected as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, recognised alongside other ancient beech forests across Europe.

It suits travellers interested in genuine wilderness and biodiversity rather than well-trodden trails. Because it's remote, check local access and go with a guide for longer excursions.

Shkodër

Shkodër isn't in the mountains, but it's the gateway to them. It's one of Albania's oldest cities, and most trips into the Alps start or end here, whether you're heading to Theth by road or to Valbona via the Koman Lake ferry.

Spend a day here before or after the mountains. There are historic sights, restaurants and a genuine city atmosphere that makes a good contrast with a few days in the villages.

Best Time to Visit the Albanian Alps

Late spring through early autumn, roughly June to September, is the main hiking season, with green valleys and long daylight hours. Many sources recommend this window specifically for Valbona.

Summer is the busiest period, so book guesthouses ahead, especially for Theth and Valbona in July and August. By early October the crowds thin out and the valleys turn gold, though some guesthouses start closing for the season.

Winter (roughly November to April) is a different landscape entirely, with peaks under snow. It can be striking, but the road into Theth usually closes, guesthouses shut, and trails become genuinely hazardous. If you're set on a winter trip, check road, weather and trail conditions carefully before you go, and don't expect the same access as in summer.

Planning Your Albanian Alps Trip

The Albanian Alps offer one of the most rewarding mountain trips in the Balkans, from the traditional village of Theth and the Grunas and Blue Eye day walks, to the Valbona Valley, the Valbona Pass trek, and quieter spots like Vermosh, Lëpushë and Razëm. Maja e Jezercës and the Gashi River Valley are there for those who want to go further off the beaten path.

Whether you're planning a short mountain getaway or a longer multi-day trek, give the trip enough time. Roads are slow, trails take longer than they look on a map, and the valleys reward a relaxed pace far more than a rushed one.
