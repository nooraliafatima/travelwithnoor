10 Best Free Things to Do in Prague

Discover historic landmarks, charming streets, beautiful parks and scenic river views without spending a fortune.

Prague, the capital of the Czech Republic, is a wonderful destination for travellers who love history, beautiful architecture and charming streets. Its medieval landmarks, riverside views and historic neighbourhoods create plenty of opportunities to explore without spending much money. From walking across Charles Bridge to relaxing in peaceful city parks, there are many free things to do in Prague that make a visit memorable.

Whether you are planning a romantic getaway, a family holiday or a solo adventure, you can enjoy much of Prague's character through its public squares, outdoor landmarks and scenic walking routes. Many of the city's most recognisable sights can be admired without purchasing an admission ticket, although certain towers, museums and historic interiors charge entry fees.

Travellers arranging their European holiday can also explore Prague tour packages for help planning their trip. Combining paid attractions with free outdoor experiences is a practical way to make the most of your time and budget.

Free Things to Do in Prague

1. Walk Across Charles Bridge

Charles Bridge is one of Prague's most recognisable landmarks. Built in the 14th century, this historic stone bridge connects the Old Town with Malá Strana across the Vltava River. Its statues, traditional architecture and views of the city make it a highlight of many Prague itineraries.

Walking across the bridge is free, and there are plenty of opportunities to take photographs along the way. Visit early in the morning for a quieter atmosphere, or arrive in the evening to admire the illuminated skyline. Allow extra time to appreciate the river views rather than simply crossing from one side to the other.

2. Explore Old Town Square

Old Town Square is at the heart of historic Prague. Colourful buildings, impressive churches and cobbled streets surround the lively public square, making it an excellent starting point for exploring the city on foot.

Look for the Jan Hus Memorial, admire the exterior of the Church of Our Lady before Týn and watch the famous Astronomical Clock on the Old Town Hall. You can enjoy the square and its architecture without paying an admission fee. However, visiting the Old Town Hall tower or entering certain attractions requires a ticket.

3. Discover Prague Castle's Courtyards

Prague Castle is a historic complex overlooking the city, with grand courtyards, impressive buildings and views across the red rooftops. It is one of the best places to appreciate Prague's long history and architectural character.

You can admire the castle's exterior and explore areas that are open to the public without an interior sightseeing ticket. However, access arrangements can vary, and some buildings, exhibitions and visitor areas require paid admission. Check the official visitor information before travelling, particularly during busy periods or special events.

4. Enjoy Views from Letná Park

Letná Park is a popular green space on a hill above the Vltava River. It is especially well known for its views of Prague's bridges, riverbanks and historic skyline. The elevated setting makes it an appealing stop for photographers and travellers who want a break from busy streets.

Follow the walking paths, find a quiet place to sit or bring snacks for an inexpensive picnic. Sunset can be a lovely time to visit, although the park is worth exploring throughout the day. It is also a good place to see residents enjoying their everyday routines away from the busiest tourist areas.

5. Visit the John Lennon Wall

The John Lennon Wall in Malá Strana is known for its colourful graffiti, artwork and messages associated with peace and freedom. Over the years, it has become a recognisable public landmark and a popular photography stop.

You can view the wall without buying an entrance ticket. Its appearance changes as new artwork and messages are added, so each visit can look a little different. Take photographs respectfully, avoid damaging the wall and follow any rules displayed at the site.

6. Walk Through Petřín Gardens

Petřín Hill offers a green escape from the busy centre of Prague. Its outdoor paths, landscaped gardens and viewpoints make it a pleasant place to spend a few hours without paying for a major attraction.

Walk uphill at a comfortable pace, enjoy views across the city and look for seasonal flowers and changing colours. Spring brings fresh greenery and blossoms, while autumn can transform the surrounding landscape with warm shades. Remember that the Petřín Observation Tower and the mirror maze have separate admission fees, and access to particular facilities may depend on the season.

7. Explore Historic Vyšehrad

Vyšehrad is a historic hilltop area that offers a different atmosphere from Prague's busiest central sights. Its outdoor grounds, old fortifications, monuments and views over the Vltava River make it a rewarding destination for travellers interested in history and peaceful walks.

Spend time exploring the accessible grounds and following the paths along the historic walls. The area is also a good option for visitors who want to experience another side of Prague beyond the main Old Town attractions. Entry fees and access rules may apply to individual buildings or exhibitions, so check before visiting.

8. Wander Around Malá Strana

Malá Strana, also known as the Lesser Town, is one of Prague's most charming historic neighbourhoods. Its narrow cobbled streets, colourful facades and traditional architecture make it ideal for a leisurely walking tour.

Take your time exploring the lanes, admiring the buildings and discovering quieter corners away from the main crowds. You can also walk towards Kampa Island for attractive riverside scenery. Exploring on foot lets you appreciate the neighbourhood without paying for a guided tour, although individual museums and interiors may charge admission.

9. Relax at Stromovka Park

Stromovka Park is a spacious urban green area that offers a relaxing change of pace from sightseeing. Its lawns, ponds and walking paths make it suitable for families, couples and solo travellers looking for an outdoor break.

Visit during spring or summer to enjoy the greenery, or come in autumn to see the changing leaves. Bring a picnic, take a gentle walk or simply find a quiet bench to rest. It is an easy way to add some nature to your Prague itinerary without increasing your travel costs.

10. Enjoy a Riverside Walk

A walk along the Vltava River is another simple way to enjoy Prague for free. The riverbanks offer views of historic bridges, passing boats and the city's distinctive skyline. Different sections of the waterfront provide different perspectives, so you can choose a route that suits your schedule.

Combine your walk with a visit to Charles Bridge or Kampa Island, or simply stroll along the river and stop for photographs. Late afternoon and sunset can provide attractive light for pictures. You do not need a boat tour to appreciate the river scenery from the public walking areas.

Tips for Exploring Prague on a Budget

A little planning can help you enjoy Prague's free attractions while keeping daily expenses manageable. Consider these practical suggestions before heading out.

  • Wear comfortable shoes: Cobblestone streets, hills and long walking routes make comfortable footwear important.
  • Start early: Popular landmarks such as Charles Bridge and Old Town Square can be quieter earlier in the day.
  • Carry water and snacks: Having refreshments with you can help reduce unplanned spending during sightseeing.
  • Group nearby attractions: Visit places in the same neighbourhood together to save time and reduce transport costs.
  • Check opening and access rules: Some courtyards, gardens and historic buildings may have temporary restrictions or separate admission charges.
  • Keep some flexibility: Leave time to explore side streets, public spaces and unexpected viewpoints along your route.
Planning tip: Free outdoor attractions are a great way to explore Prague, but always check current access information for historic sites and paid interiors before your visit.

Conclusion

Prague proves that an enjoyable European holiday does not have to involve expensive activities every day. From crossing Charles Bridge and exploring Old Town Square to admiring the views from Letná Park and walking through Malá Strana, the city offers plenty of memorable experiences that cost nothing.

The best way to enjoy these attractions is to combine nearby places, allow time for walking and balance sightseeing with breaks in parks and public spaces. Keep in mind that some buildings, towers and exhibitions have separate entrance fees, even when their surrounding outdoor areas are free to explore.
