UAE National Day 2026: Dates, Events and Travel Guide

Discover the holiday dates, celebrations, cultural attractions and travel tips to help you plan your UAE National Day break.

Dubai skyline during an evening visit

UAE National Day, also known as Eid Al Etihad, falls on Wednesday 2 and Thursday 3 December 2026, marking 55 years since the country was founded in 1971. Here is when it is, what happens, and how to plan a trip around it.

Whether you want fireworks in Dubai, a mosque visit in Abu Dhabi or a mountain drive in the north, a little planning pays off. National Day tour packages from Dubai can help with transport and bookings if you would rather not organise everything yourself. Keep an eye on official announcements for event schedules and road updates.

When Is UAE National Day 2026?

National Day falls on 2 December every year. In 2026, that date is a Wednesday, and the public holiday covers 2 and 3 December. Whether it becomes a longer break depends on your employer's working week, so check with them and watch for any official changes to the holiday schedule.

Dubai's 2026 retail calendar lists Eid Al Etihad celebrations from 30 November to 3 December, with fireworks, cultural showcases, promotions and family activities. Venue schedules usually appear closer to the event, so treat individual fireworks locations and show times as unconfirmed until organisers publish them.

Why the UAE Celebrates National Day

The UAE was formally proclaimed a sovereign state on 2 December 1971. Six emirates, Abu Dhabi, Dubai, Sharjah, Ajman, Umm Al Quwain and Fujairah, agreed to form the federation, with Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan as its founding father. Ras Al Khaimah joined on 10 February 1972, completing the seven emirates.

Today, the day is marked with Emirati music and dance, heritage displays, national colours and community events run by schools, businesses and public bodies. For visitors, it is a good chance to experience Emirati hospitality and traditions.

What to See and Do

Fireworks and Light Shows

Fireworks are a highlight for many families. In previous years, Dubai displays have taken place at Global Village, The Beach at JBR, Bluewaters Island, Dubai Festival City Mall, Souk Al Seef and Riverland at Dubai Parks and Resorts. The 2026 locations and times should be confirmed through official announcements before you travel.

Arrive early, allow extra time for road closures, and agree on a meeting point with your group in case you get separated in the crowd. Read the venue's entry rules beforehand so you know what to expect.

Emirati Heritage and Culture

National Day programmes often include traditional dances such as Al Ayyala and Al Harbiya, folk music, crafts, Arabic calligraphy and local food. Heritage venues in Dubai, including Al Shindagha Museum and the Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood, have hosted cultural programmes during National Day celebrations. Hatta Heritage Village has also held cultural activities in previous years.

The Etihad Museum, beside Union House where the federation was declared, is another worthwhile stop. Check each venue's opening hours and event announcements, as schedules may change during public holidays.

Dubai's Landmarks

The holiday is a good time to visit Dubai's famous attractions. Enjoy views of the Burj Khalifa, stroll around Dubai Marina, explore the old souks or spend time along the waterfront. Malls and entertainment venues may also arrange special shows and family events.

Popular locations can become crowded during the holiday. Book activities ahead of time and group your plans by area to reduce unnecessary travel across the city.

Take a Day Trip to Abu Dhabi

The Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque is an important stop for visitors interested in architecture and culture. Opening hours, visitor arrangements, entry requirements and tour availability can change, particularly on public holidays. Check the mosque's official visitor information before setting out rather than relying on previously published timings or fees.

Visitors should dress respectfully. Women should be prepared to cover their hair and wear loose clothing that covers their arms and legs. Men should also wear clothing that meets the mosque's dress requirements. Check the current visitor rules for details about acceptable clothing and available facilities.

Other options include Al Maqta Fort, Heritage Village, the Saadiyat Cultural District and attractions on Yas Island. The Sheikh Zayed Festival at Al Wathba is another event to watch for when the programme is announced.

If you are driving back to Dubai on the same day, plan your route first and expect heavier traffic, tighter parking and possible security arrangements around major sites.

Where Else to Go: The Other Emirates

Dubai is a good base for architecture, shopping, dining and entertainment, but the other emirates offer different experiences. Choose destinations based on your interests and the time you have.

Sharjah: Museums and Heritage

Sharjah is known for museums, cultural attractions and family outings. Al Qasba, Al Majaz Waterfront and the Arabian Wildlife Centre are options to consider. Khorfakkan, on the east coast, offers a beach, a waterfront and an amphitheatre.

Ras Al Khaimah: Mountains and Outdoor Activities

Ras Al Khaimah is worth considering if you prefer mountains, outdoor adventures and scenic drives. Jebel Jais is a major attraction for mountain views and adventure activities, while Dhayah Fort offers a glimpse into the emirate's history. Check activity availability and operating conditions before visiting.

Fujairah: East Coast Scenery

Fujairah is an option for travellers who want to explore the UAE's east coast. Possible stops include Fujairah Corniche, Ain Al Madhab Gardens, Wadi Wurayah and Al Aqah Beach near Dibba. Check access restrictions and local conditions before visiting natural areas.

Do not try to cover every emirate in a few days. A two- or three-day trip with shorter drives can be more enjoyable than an itinerary spent mostly on the road. If you are travelling with children, combine one major attraction with something relaxed, such as a beach or park.

Travel Tips for UAE National Day 2026

Book Early

Hotels can fill up and popular restaurants can become busy. Check cancellation policies and whether attractions require advance tickets. If you are travelling with young children or older relatives, consider staying close to the main sights you plan to visit.

Follow Official Rules and Announcements

Watch for official notices about fireworks, concerts, road closures and public transport changes. Do not stop in no-parking zones for photographs. Fireworks should only be handled by authorised organisers.

Authorities may impose restrictions on vehicle decorations, loud music, road obstruction and unsafe behaviour during celebrations. Follow current police and government instructions rather than relying on old social media posts.

Pack for December

Days can be pleasant for sightseeing, but evenings may feel cool, particularly in the desert and mountains. Bring a light jacket, comfortable shoes, water and any essentials your children need. Dress modestly at religious and cultural sites and respect photography rules.

Sort Out Your Travel Documents

Visa rules depend on your passport. Some nationalities can enter visa-free, while others need a visa or must meet additional conditions. Confirm the latest requirements with the Federal Authority for Identity, Citizenship, Customs and Port Security or Dubai's General Directorate of Residency and Foreigners Affairs before travelling.

Check your passport validity and keep digital copies of important bookings, identification documents and travel insurance details. Travellers coming from abroad should confirm entry requirements before making non-refundable arrangements.

Budgeting for a National Day Trip

Your costs depend on how many places you visit, ticket prices, food, transport and accommodation. A day trip from Dubai saves on a hotel but may involve fuel, parking and tolls. Staying overnight adds accommodation costs, and holiday rates can be higher than usual.

Set a realistic budget that includes meals, parking, shopping and small extras. Look for official promotions, family tickets and bundled deals, but read the conditions and compare the total cost before deciding whether an offer represents good value.

If you prefer to organise less of the trip yourself, compare available tours and activities before making a decision. Check what is included, the cancellation terms and whether transport is part of the arrangement.

Conclusion

UAE National Day 2026 is an opportunity to learn about the country's history, enjoy Emirati traditions and explore the seven emirates. Whether you like fireworks, culture, landmarks or time with family, you can plan a holiday around your interests.

Book early, check official event announcements and allow extra time for travel during busy periods. Be realistic about how much you can fit into your itinerary, whether you prefer the energy of the city or the quieter mountains and coast.

Comparing Dubai National Day holiday packages can help you review available tours, accommodation and sightseeing options. With a flexible plan and a little preparation, the holiday can be a relaxed mix of culture, rest and fun.
